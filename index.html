Racing the Clock

Teaching VLNs Budget-Conditioned Object Search

Best Paper Award, IROS 2026 Workshop on Intelligent Information Gathering

An indoor search robot must split its budget between nearby targets and distant rooms. A short budget may favor immediate collection, while a longer one can justify travelling to a promising room.

We study budget-conditioned object search: finding as many distinct instances of a requested category as possible within an action budget. The policy reads its remaining budget as a countdown in the instruction.

Changing only the stated budget changes the behaviour of the policy.

The model is rolled out from the same start pose in the same held-out HM3D scene with identical deterministic decoding. Only the budget in the instruction changes. The trajectories differ substantially rather than simply extending one another, most visibly between 150 and 400 steps.

A stronger expert is not necessarily a better teacher.

As an expert, the object-level planner leads at every budget. After both are distilled into the same VLN under matched training conditions, the order reverses: room-level supervision achieves the highest mean collection among evaluated VLNs at every tested budget.

At 2000 steps it finds 72.7% of reachable targets, versus 68.5% under greedy-frontier supervision, 65.5% under object-level supervision and 44.8% for the pretrained VLN.

Two experts from one Bellman recursion, differing in what they know.

We cast expert design as a choice of scene abstraction. A single budget-indexed Bellman recursion, applied to different abstractions of the full environment, yields experts that differ in what they know.

Object-level Bellman expert

Given every target location. Search reduces to budgeted routing among known targets: it has nothing left to search for.

Room-level Bellman expert (ours)

Retains observed room connectivity, doorway evidence and cross-scene semantic priors, but withholds the contents of unexplored rooms. It must search for targets that its student also cannot see.

Method

01The task

An episode gives the agent a multi-room environment, a start pose, a target category and an action budget B. At each step the policy picks a primitive action from its RGB history, the instruction and the remaining budget. It never sees a complete map or the locations of unseen targets.

The reward counts each target instance once, when it is first credited by camera field of view and proximity. The goal is to collect as many distinct instances as possible before the budget runs out. Changing B changes the decision problem, not just the point at which a fixed trajectory is cut off.

02One Bellman recursion over a scene abstraction

An expert does not plan over the full environment. It plans over an abstraction of it, and knows only the part of that abstraction revealed so far. For remaining budget b, both experts solve the same recursion:

Qφ⋆(s,b,a)=Rφ(s,a)+𝔼s′[Vφ⋆(s′,b−c(a))] Vφ⋆(s,b)=maxa:c(a)≤bQφ⋆(s,b,a),Vφ⋆(s,0)=0

The experts differ in what the abstraction retains and in how much of it is revealed. That choice sets the uncertainty each one has to reason over.

03Distilling the experts into a VLN

Expert demonstrations are converted to Uni-NaVid's native training format. Each record holds the video history, the expert's next four primitives and a natural-language countdown of the steps remaining. The budget is provided only through language, with no numerical budget input and no architectural change.

The room-level supervision has 388,199 records from 89 HM3D and 59 Matterport3D scenes, at budgets of 50, 150, 400, 800, 1200 and 2000 steps. The object-level, room-level and greedy-frontier students use the same scenes, budgets and record count, so only the expert supervision differs.

Zero-shot on a quadruped in a multi-room home.

We deploy the room-level Bellman-supervised VLN on an indoor quadruped without real-world fine-tuning. An onboard camera streams RGB to a remote GPU server, which returns four primitives per call in 0.22 s on average.

In a home containing 14 chairs, we fix the start pose, target and weights and vary only the budget, with five runs per budget. Mean discoveries rise from 3.4 to 9.2 as the budget increases.
